Ramp Slope Formula (%): Slope (%) = (Rise / Horizontal Run) × 100

US Standard Example (ADA 1:12): To overcome a vertical rise of 12 inches (1 ft) with a horizontal run of 144 inches (12 ft), the slope is: (12 / 144) × 100 = 8.33% (the maximum slope permitted under ADA Section 405).

Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ
What is the maximum legal slope for a wheelchair ramp under ADA Standards?
Under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA Standards Section 405.2), the maximum allowable slope for any ramp on an accessible route is 1:12 (8.33% grade), which equals 1 inch of vertical rise for every 12 inches of horizontal run. The maximum single continuous rise permitted is 30 inches (760 mm) before an intermediate level landing is required. A gentler slope of 1:16 to 1:20 (5% to 6.25%) is strongly recommended for effortless manual wheelchair propulsion.
When are level landings, handrails, and edge protection mandatory?
Under ADA Sec. 405.7 and IBC Section 1012:
  • Landings: Mandatory at the top and bottom of each ramp run, and at every 30 inches of vertical rise. Landings must be at least 60 inches long by 60 inches wide if the ramp changes direction.
  • Handrails: Mandatory on both sides for any ramp run with a vertical rise greater than 6 inches (150 mm). Height must be between 34 and 38 inches above the walking surface.
  • Edge Protection: Ramps and landings with drop-offs must provide continuous curbs at least 2 inches (50 mm) high or extended floor surfaces to prevent wheelchair casters from slipping off.
How do you calculate ramp run length from vertical rise?
The mathematical formula is: Horizontal Run = Rise / (Slope % / 100). For an ADA compliant 1:12 ramp (8.33%), simply multiply your total vertical rise in inches by 12. For example, overcoming an 18-inch porch rise requires: 18 × 12 = 216 inches (18 feet) of continuous ground run.
What is the maximum slope for residential and commercial vehicle driveways?
Under IBC Section 406 and municipal codes, vehicle driveway ramps are generally limited to a maximum grade of 15% to 20% for straight sections, and 12% to 15% for curved ramps. To avoid scraping low-clearance vehicle bumpers, codes mandate a transition slope blend (minimum 8 to 10 feet long at half the main slope) at the top crest and bottom sag.
