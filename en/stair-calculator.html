Frequently Asked Questions about Stair Rise, Run & Blondel Formula

What is Blondel's formula for stair comfort?
Architect François Blondel established that the biomechanical relationship for walking up stairs without fatigue is: 2 × Riser + 1 × Tread = 24 ÷ 25.5 inches (62 ÷ 64 cm) (ideal value 25 inches / 63 cm). This reproduces a person's natural horizontal stride length.
What are standard residential rise and run dimensions according to the IRC?
Under the International Residential Code (IRC Section R311.7), the maximum riser height is 7.75 inches (19.7 cm) and the minimum tread depth is 10.0 inches (25.4 cm) with at least a 3/4-in nosing (or 11.0 in without nosing).
How many vertical feet or risers can you build before needing a landing?
Building safety codes (IRC & IBC) require a rest landing for every 12 feet (144 inches or approx. 18 risers) of vertical rise. The landing length in the direction of travel must be at least equal to the stair width (typically 36 inches minimum).
What is the ideal angle of incline for comfortable stairs?
The optimal angle of inclination for interior residential stairs is between 30° and 37°. Steeper stairs exceeding 42° become hazardous to descend, while slopes under 26° act more like stepped ramps.
